Binance says it has made users whole after the largest hack in crypto

November 12, 2026 · CryptoTerminal Daily · Thread on X

Binance has told customers that the repayment is finished. Two weeks after the Oct. 29 breach that cost it $6.34B, the exchange announced on Thursday that it has completed coverage of the assets affected. That is a milestone, but the way it was reached and what remains unproven say more about the state of crypto security than the headline does.

What Binance actually did

The exchange did not recover anything from the attackers. It paid. According to Bitcoin Magazine, Binance committed more than $6.34B of its own money and purchased the affected tokens, some on open markets and some in private deals. The funding came from three places: a partial deployment of SAFU, the Secure Asset Fund for Users, a portion of its futures insurance fund that sat above the level its risk framework requires, and the corporate treasury.

The repayment came in stages. Binance pledged to cover customer losses within hours of the breach, then reported coverage of 90% and 95% as withdrawals began to reopen, and later said 98% of affected tokens had been replenished. The last slice was held in less liquid assets that took longer to buy. Thursday's statement is the first time the exchange has said the process is done.

A small number of affected tokens are no longer listed on Binance. Those holdings will go through its delisting process, and the exchange says holders will be covered there too.

The SAFU question

SAFU exists for exactly this kind of day, and it has been drawn down. Binance says it will rebuild the fund to about $1B. That commitment matters because the fund is meant to be an emergency reserve for users. A reserve that was tapped and not refilled would leave the next incident exposed.

The proof is not ready yet

Alongside the announcement Binance published a new proof-of-reserves snapshot, taken at 00:00 UTC on Nov. 12. It covers 198 tokens. The earlier snapshot, published on Oct. 31 in the days after the breach, covered only 10. The jump is large and it is the best evidence of scale the exchange has offered.

But customers cannot check it yet. Binance says the complete zero-knowledge proof needs roughly 40 to 50 hours of computation. Only after that can holders of the affected tokens confirm on their own that every asset is backed one to one at least. Until then, the claim of full backing is Binance's statement and nothing more. That is not an accusation. It is simply the difference between a promise and something anyone can audit.

Withdrawals are also still returning in phases rather than all at once, so many users are still waiting for the normal service they had before Oct. 29.

The bigger picture

The Binance case looks different when set against the rest of the year. ARK Investment Management's research director Lorenzo Valente argues that crypto hackers have now stolen $8.7B in 2026, more than the $5.6B in onchain revenue he estimates for the whole year. Since 2017, he says, the industry has lost about $26.9B to hacks while earning $47B onchain. His summary on X was blunt: "Security is the bottleneck."

Binance's single theft is larger than his estimate for the full year of industry revenue. Other incidents he lists include a $387M hack at Bitget in September, a $320M exploit of the Liquid Network and a $295M exploit of Drift Protocol in April.

The effect is visible in DeFi. According to DefiLlama, total value locked in decentralized finance has dropped nearly 10% since the Binance exploit, from $85.9B on Oct. 29 to $77.4B on Thursday. Cointelegraph notes that the string of attacks is weighing on institutional appetite for DeFi.

Why it matters

Binance is one of the few firms in crypto with the balance sheet to absorb a loss of this size and still tell customers they lost nothing. That is a strength, and it likely kept a bad event from becoming a run on the exchange. It is also an uncomfortable fact for everyone else. Most protocols and smaller exchanges have no equivalent of SAFU and no treasury that could cover a nine-figure loss, let alone one in the billions.

The uncomfortable arithmetic from the ARK numbers is that hacks are now a large cost relative to what the sector earns. If that trend holds through year-end, losses to hacks will exceed onchain revenue for the first time since 2020.

What to watch

Two things will tell us whether the repayment story holds. The first is the zero-knowledge proof, due in about two days. If it backs the 198-token snapshot, users get a verification they can run themselves and the repayment claim becomes far stronger. The second is the pace of the withdrawal queues, which will show how quickly normal operations return.

A third question has no date attached: whether the stolen funds can be traced and frozen. Binance has paid, but the attackers still hold the coins. Coverage ended the customer problem. It did not end the theft.
