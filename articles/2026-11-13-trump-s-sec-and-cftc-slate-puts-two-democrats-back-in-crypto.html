Trump's SEC and CFTC Slate Puts Two Democrats Back in Crypto's Rulemaking Seats

November 13, 2026 · CryptoTerminal Daily · Thread on X

Four names, two agencies, one narrow window

On Thursday the White House sent four nominations to the Senate, two for the Securities and Exchange Commission and two for the Commodity Futures Trading Commission. Each agency gets one Republican and one Democrat. It is the first time this administration has put forward Democrats for either commission, and the first batch of nominees for these seats in roughly a year.

The names matter less than the arithmetic behind them. The SEC currently has two of its five seats filled, held by Chairman Paul Atkins and Commissioner Mark Uyeda. The CFTC has one: Chairman Michael Selig sits alone on a five-seat commission. Both agencies can seat no more than three commissioners from a single party, so a full bench would be a mixed one by design.

Who is being nominated

Steven Levine, a Republican, is slated for the SEC seat vacated by Hester Peirce, with a term running through June 5, 2030. He most recently worked as general counsel and senior adviser at the White House Council of Economic Advisers and before that held a staff role at the SEC under Commissioner Mark Uyeda.

Kristin Johnson, a Democrat, would take the seat of Caroline Crenshaw, with a term ending June 5, 2029. Johnson is a former law school professor who served as a CFTC commissioner until 2025.

At the CFTC, Nathan Anonick, a Republican, is up for the slot formerly occupied by ex-Chairman Rostin Behnam, with a term that runs to June 19, 2031. Matthew Rowland, a Democrat, would take the remainder of former Commissioner Summer Mersinger's term, which expires April 13, 2028.

The Clarity Act connection

Both CFTC nominees come out of the Senate Agriculture Committee, which will also vet them. Anonick advises committee Chairman John Boozman as legislative counsel, and he helped negotiate the committee's share of the crypto market-structure bill known as the Clarity Act. Rowland has spent his career as a CFTC lawyer, on loan to the committee, and he too worked on that bill as well as a 2024 crypto measure. He previously served on Johnson's staff at the CFTC.

That bill matters as the backdrop. It failed a Senate cloture vote, 49 to 50, on Sept. 15 and expires on Jan. 3 unless the lame-duck session revives it. With no new law, the two regulators have filled the gap with their own authority. The CFTC has cleared perpetual futures on individual stocks and floated bringing crypto exchanges under federal oversight. The SEC has issued an innovation exemption for tokenized stocks and guidance suggesting most crypto assets are not securities. Industry now gets its clarity from agencies instead of Congress, and a fully staffed pair of commissions would make that arrangement more durable.

Two seats that could swing in opposite directions

The most interesting detail is who each nominee replaces. Peirce led the SEC's Crypto Task Force and was one of the loudest voices for clearer crypto rules. Levine's own views on digital assets are not well established in public, which makes his future votes a real variable for whether the SEC's friendly posture holds.

Crenshaw, by contrast, was a frequent skeptic of the industry. Johnson spoke often about custody and decentralized finance during her CFTC tenure and pressed for stronger customer protections and conflict-of-interest rules. Her return, this time at the SEC, would add a voice that leans toward consumer safeguards.

Rowland's enforcement-heavy background, combined with his party, could introduce a dissenting voice to a CFTC that under Selig has leaned firmly toward the industry. Selig himself called the pair two strong bipartisan picks and said he looks forward to working with them if they are confirmed.

Why the pairing looks deliberate

After the Nov. 3 midterms, in which Democrats won both chambers, analyst Jaret Seiberg of TD Cowen warned clients that Democrats were unlikely to wave through any Trump nominee unless Democratic seats were also filled, at the SEC for example. He considered that improbable. Thursday's slate does exactly that at both regulators.

Senator Elizabeth Warren, one of crypto's sharpest critics, is expected to chair the Banking Committee once the new Congress is seated in January. That committee handles the SEC picks, and CoinDesk reports it has already moved quickly, setting the nominees for review next week. The Agriculture Committee will take the CFTC pair before any vote by the full Senate, where confirmation requires a majority.

What to watch

No timetable has come from the White House. What remains unclear is whether a Senate about to flip parties will clear all four names during the lame-duck weeks, or whether both regulators limp into 2027 with skeleton staffing.

Three things will tell us which way it goes. First, whether Banking's early scheduling produces a vote or only a hearing. Second, how Levine answers questions about the crypto work Peirce led. Third, whether Democrats treat the slate as a fair trade or as a way to lock in a pro-industry agenda before they take over.

Our read is that the pairing was built to be confirmable, which is unusual for crypto-relevant appointments. Earlier, the White House withdrew Brian Quintenz as its pick for CFTC chair after a dispute that drew in the Winklevoss twins, so the year-long gap reflected real turbulence. If the four are confirmed, the commissions that have driven crypto policy since the Clarity Act collapsed would finally have the votes and staying power to turn proposals into durable rules, and a dissenting voice inside each one.
